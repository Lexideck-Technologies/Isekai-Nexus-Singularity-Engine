# Resolution rules

Present fictional results, useful information, and player choices.

## Execute one causal result

Read the accepted character, current world, actual encounter rank, personal system terms, and incoming [scene state](scene-state.md). Resolve the declared action dicelessly through narrative causality, actual ability access, preparation, competence, and tier interaction. The action occurs; ineffective damage remains an ineffective damage component with a useful consequence, information, opening, negotiation, or escape route. Do not turn the source's extreme-gap examples into compulsory injury. Apply their logic to the actual situation. Honor powerful abilities and conditional advantages when their terms apply. Do not add a complication or resource cost merely because the procedure uses Yes-And or Yes-But.

Adjudicate all costs. Commit physical outcomes, HP/MP changes, damage, conditions, and elapsed time. Send inventory/funds movements to Craft-Trade/Economy for its single commitment; never decrement those resources independently. Record one attempt identity and separate effect identities. Read previously applied identities before changing state. Determine gains only after completing the turn's actions. Send earned evidence to Progression and other owners, then commit their returned effects once. A character cannot receive another victory, loot grant, or resource charge because Academy, Cultivation, Hunter, or another loop also covered the action.

Use existing HP/MP, damage, cost, and recovery rules. Source provides attribute domains, not universal starting-stat, HP/MP, damage, or initiative formulas. Preserve descriptive outcomes if numbers are absent. Establish an accepted campaign calibration before introducing a numeric formula; never invent a source formula. Do not infer a loss of all MP from one source calibration example.

First tier-appropriate encounters showcase actual protagonist competence: one-shot appropriate foes, including a goblin group that normally takes a party of four. Cheats give their intended unfair advantage; modern-knowledge traps and Earth logic work spectacularly. Cheats, informed preparation, and Earth knowledge have their promised advantage. A higher-tier entity keeps its established defenses; give a feasible approach, ally, special property, bargain, or return path rather than quietly weakening the player's defining power. Foe names are illustrative; the world instance and encounter's real capabilities determine its rank.

## Shared rank authority

The tables below own threat, encounter EXP range, and encounter loot rank. Progression consumes them; other loops reference them. Use the recorded encounter rank for all three. A goblin named in the F examples is an F encounter; the default Goblin/Orc roster entry is D. Species name alone does not override actual encounter rank. A dragon in an A example differs from the AA Elder Dragon. Record a deliberate variant's capabilities and rank before resolution. This resolves source example/roster disagreement without changing either source entry.

Loot items are tier examples, not automatic promises that every creature carries that exact item. Select world-compatible recoverable loot with the matching rank and send the identified acquisition to Craft-Trade/Economy for commitment once. Item consumption or use invokes its actual terms. A loot item's advertised potential does not independently award a second level or conceptual tier. S+ encounter results pass to conceptual advancement; do not convert nonnumeric rewards into an invented EXP amount.

Retain the source's party split rule: experience divides among all active members; Growth Boost applies to the protagonist's share only (`PARTY.RULES`). No equal-share formula or Growth Boost multiplier is supplied. Record the campaign's actual allocation, participants, and promised multiplier if established; do not fabricate one. Social/Companion owns companion affinity modifiers and eligibility, not this table.

## Noncombat adaptation

Campaign default: award completed meaningful noncombat challenges through the same encounter-rank EXP range once. A practical examination, rescue, research breakthrough, invention, exploration objective, or nonviolent resolution qualifies through demonstrated accomplishment. Determine its observed challenge rank from actual difficulty, scope, opposition, resources, and world conditions; select and record a justified award within that rank's range. Record the achievement, rank, award basis, and effect identity. Use this default for meaningful noncombat accomplishments. Ordinary play requires no extra XP-convention question.

Ordinary attendance and repetition of an already-solved exercise earn no automatic EXP. Distinct evidence of improved technique, control, or integration can advance the relevant skill or cultivation state. One successful activity can justify capacity and control gains with distinct effects, never duplicate EXP. Accepted personal-system reward terms override the default for that character; honor extraordinary training promises under their stated conditions. Do not manufacture hostile encounters to permit growth. Existing accepted campaign reward methods remain authoritative for that campaign.

## Action and tier interaction

Resolve each declared action as success with an appropriate consequence or cost. Never negate it with "you can't do that", "that doesn't work", silent omission, or a GM veto. Extreme tier gaps, actions requiring a narrative cost, and earned reversals produce an action that occurred plus a consequence that advances the situation. Success of the action does not mean unrestricted success against a target's defenses. A mortal strike can bounce from an S entity and gain its attention; a resisted spell can reveal an opening for retreat or a clever gambit.

| Target above actor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Same tier | Standard full damage/outcome |
| One tier | Half effect; status effects have low probability |
| Two tiers | Approximately 10% effect; spells almost always resisted |
| Three tiers | Near immunity; only Defense-Piercing or Absolute properties inflict minor damage |
| Four or more tiers | Total nullification; possible backlash or reflection |
| S target against mortal ability | Fundamentally ineffective without divine artifact, World Item, or Authority |

Consult [Character Rules](character-rules.md) for rank bands and [Progression Rules](progression-rules.md) for specific S/SS/SSS defenses. Probability descriptions above are qualitative; source supplies no numeric chance for low probability or resistance.

## Encounter award spine

| Rank | Eligible encounter advancement | Matching loot example and effect |
|---|---|---|
| F | 1-10 EXP; bulk beginner encounters | Beast Bones: common crafting material |
| D | 20-150 EXP; low-level career | Health Potion: moderate HP restoration |
| C | 200-1,000 EXP; competent adventurer | Magic Sword: well-crafted fire/ice enchantment |
| B | 1,500-10,000 EXP; noticeable mid-tier gain | Ring of Regeneration: constant passive HP regeneration |
| A | 15,000-100,000 EXP; potential tier advance | Dragon's Heart: legendary forging or consumption for immense power |
| AA | 250,000-1,500,000 EXP; potential cap break or multiple mastered skills | Philosopher's Stone: matter transmutation, immortality elixirs, immense magic amplification |
| AAA | 5,000,000-25,000,000 EXP; final-boss scale, mortal pinnacle | God's Divine Core: crystallized dead-god essence granting part of divine authority |
| S | Variable nonnumeric Title, Divine Blessing, or System Authority | World Item: whole-world effects, weather alteration or absolute wish |
| SS | World-altering event granting World Item or domain Authority, such as Storms | Akashic Records: metaphysical library of past/present/future multiverse knowledge |
| SSS | Survival grants forbidden knowledge, Cosmic Insight or Shard of Cosmic Truth; no conventional Outer God kill | Seed of a New Universe: wielder becomes creator god of a new universe |

Mortal F-AAA numerical awards feed level advancement. S+ results feed conceptual catalysts. Item acquisition and advancement evidence remain distinct owned effects even when one event supplies both.


## Commit ownership

| Effect | Owner |
|---|---|
| Outcome, health/MP, physical conditions, time | Resolution |
| Items acquired, consumed or transferred; funds spent or earned | Craft-Trade/Economy |
| Advancement | Progression |
| Relationships and reputation | Social/Companions |
| Domain project state and income eligibility | Domain |

Use [scene state](scene-state.md) for the shared owner contract. Adjudicating a material cost proposes the Economy effect; it does not apply a separate inventory/funds subtraction. Domain eligibility does not itself deposit income. Distinct effect identities under one attempt preserve both causal agreement and single commitment.
