# Culture generator

Build cultures through causal combinations. Apply this procedure during world building and when expanding a region. Preserve established geography, institutions, history, and named peoples.

## Inputs

Use location, travel access, climate, resources, historical pressures, neighboring societies, power systems, and scale. Choose missing details to fit the world. Generate a society with internal variation and people who make individual choices.

## Construction

1. Choose an environment and its movement constraints: ocean route, river basin, continental interior, mountain chain, aerial corridor, subterranean region, or mixed frontier.
2. Connect resources to livelihoods. Define what people produce, import, repair, protect, and exchange. Give wealth and labor concrete sources.
3. Choose two historical pressures and their lasting effects: migration, pact, disaster, discovery, liberation, contested border, institutional reform, or lost route. Establish who remembers the events differently.
4. Derive institutions from recurring needs. Define authority, membership, dispute resolution, public services, and education. Give each institution a benefit, a conflict, and a way to change it.
5. Develop daily practice: food, dwellings, clothing, celebration, mourning, hospitality, artistic expression, and measures of status. Link practices to circumstances and allow regional variation.
6. Connect magical practice to livelihood and teaching. Define access, apprenticeships, costs, valued expertise, and cultivation habits. A tradition teaches a method; ancestry does not dictate talent or personality.
7. Build relationships with two neighbors: a mutually useful exchange and a specific disagreement. Give participants reasons to cooperate and room to negotiate.
8. Create internal differences through occupation, generation, locality, philosophy, and access to resources. Include a reformer, a defender of tradition, and someone whose life crosses cultural boundaries.
9. Add a playable welcome, a practical problem, a discovery, and a growth opportunity. Connect each to a defined place and person.
10. Check geographic feasibility, resources, institutions, history, magic, and neighboring relationships. Repair contradictions and preserve variety across generated cultures.

## Output frame

```text
### {Culture_Name}

{Place_History_And_Livelihood}

{Institutions_Daily_Life_And_Magical_Practice}

{Internal_Differences_And_Neighbor_Relationships}

{Named_People_And_Playable_Opportunities}
```

Use this frame inside the world's Cultures & Daily Life section. Keep procedure labels and combination choices out of the generated fiction. Generate additional cultures at the scale of a district, region, island, continent, or ocean network. Record new canon in the world draft before referencing it elsewhere.
