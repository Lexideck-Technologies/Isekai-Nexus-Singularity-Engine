# Character evaluation

Read [the graph](../isekai-nexus/references/topology.md), [intake agreement](../isekai-nexus/references/intake-contract.md), [character rules](../isekai-nexus/references/character-rules.md), and [record contract](../isekai-nexus/references/character-record.md). Read [the interface agreement](../isekai-nexus/references/interface-rules.md) and [world magic rules](../isekai-nexus/references/world-rules.md). Read [system concepts](../isekai-nexus/references/system-concepts.md) for personal variants and [alternate starts](../isekai-nexus/references/alternate-starts.md) for start adaptations. Use the complete resolved world and its selected start, not its summary card.

## Ownership

Own the draft build and its accepted revision. Preserve accepted identity, origin, motivation, world version, start, system promises, and player constraints. Arrival owns scene entry; progression owns later awards. Initial assets are starting allocations, not quest rewards. Revisiting this procedure edits or verifies the same allocation; it never grants it twice.

## Procedure

1. Check intake completion. Return only the first missing decision to Ingestion. Proceed with the complete world document and the required accepted intake decisions. For an existing traveler, load their actual record and evaluate local access without rebuilding their identity or resetting established level.
2. Read the selected world's start: current power, assets, relationships, obligations, first opportunity, and recovery path. Use concrete world-native adaptations over generic kit defaults. Flag conflicts with accepted player facts and resolve the defining choice before changing it. Do not merge kits from several starts.
3. Separate remembered expertise, usable techniques, sealed abilities, and current power. Give every seal a stated release condition. Preserve Hunter assets and working expertise; grant no destination omniscience. Use the world's new-Hunter capacity when specified. When absent, propose a capacity consistent with its pursuit and obtain acceptance. Regressors retain the selected variant's memories and assets; Time Travel does not carry future equipment unless its start explicitly permits it.
4. Derive a class and synthesis package from accepted history, motivation, world power systems, and start. Follow the shared package modes. Use one package; account for its effects once. Preserve deliberate player-defined advantages. A unique name alone grants no additional powers. Keep rare paths under their actual conditions; do not turn them into a routine selection menu.
5. Resolve the personal system into background, talent, skill, artifact, pact, interface, or distinct cooperating parts. Define each operative benefit's trigger, effect, limits, costs, growth, and condition for suspension or return. Honor its promise when conditions hold. Keep guidance and personality separate from powers. Ask one concrete choice if world compatibility requires changing the defining promise. Do not impose a penalty simply because a benefit is conditional.
6. Establish level, rank, skills and their levels, attributes, health and resource state, possessions, and relevant relationships using the shared rules and world/start facts. Use known numeric values exactly. The source supplies attribute meanings but no universal initial-stat or HP/MP formula. If the world also lacks one, use a descriptive profile, or propose a recorded campaign-specific numeric calibration when numbers are desired. State its basis and obtain acceptance before using new arithmetic. Apply explicit modifiers once; never invent a baseline silently. Fetch EXP thresholds from [Progression rules](../isekai-nexus/references/progression-rules.md) when showing numeric EXP.
7. Check compatibility: effects fit stated capacity; powerful training is not instant mastery; equipment works under its recorded access terms; talent and skill overlap cannot duplicate a benefit; cultivation and leveling remain distinct. Show usable powers and meaningful limits plainly. Preserve the desired power fantasy and offer fitting implementations for conflicts.
8. Present the concise draft record. If new substantive mechanics or obligations need acceptance, ask one clear confirmation and stop. Explicit delegation to generate and play accepts compatible generated details; it does not authorize changing fixed player facts or accepting new coercive terms. Store the accepted build in available state with world/start revision and allocation identity. Claim durable storage only after an actual write.
9. Hand the accepted record to the arrival route with the selected start's location and first opportunity. Do not narrate arrival, award EXP, create quest-completion titles, or begin a forced guild sequence during evaluation.

## Output frame

Allocate matching transport-origin benefits separately from synthesis, using the shared character rules. Do not infer an origin category from the personal system's personality. Preserve each effect's provenance and apply it once.

Use the display style suited to the world and player. Keep rule headings, procedure names, and internal state keys out of the reply. Use short prose and plain labeled values. Omit empty sections.

```text
{Character_Name_And_World_Native_Identity}
{Current_Power_And_Resource_State}
{Class_And_Usable_Abilities_With_Terms}
{Personal_System_Benefit_And_Conditions}
{Retained_Expertise_And_Recovery_Path}
{Starting_Possessions_And_Commitments}

{One_Material_Choice_Or_Confirmation_If_Required}
```

## Revision and return

Change only dependent fields. A cosmetic edit leaves allocations intact. A system-term edit rechecks its effects and package accounting. A world change rechecks compatibility, start, and local access; it preserves the accepted concept and traveler history. Suspend arrival while a defining conflict remains unresolved. Answer administrative questions and resume the pending build decision without advancing it.

Read the [Codex play contract](../isekai-nexus/references/codex-play-contract.md) when entering play or restoring a campaign after context loss.
